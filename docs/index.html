CropCure · Model documentation

Inside the CropCure disease model.

A convolutional neural network that looks at a single leaf photo and identifies one of 38 conditions across 14 crops, running entirely in the visitor's browser with TensorFlow.js. This page documents what it supports, how it is built and where the treatment advice comes from.

02 · Coverage

14 crops, 26 diseases.

    04 · Architecture

    A VGG-style CNN with 32,418,762 parameters.

    Five convolution blocks progressively shrink the 256 × 256 image to a 6 × 6 × 512 feature map, followed by a 1,500-unit dense layer and a 38-way softmax. The lime bar in each row shows its share of the parameters — the first dense layer alone holds about 85% of them, which is why the model is 124 MB.

    Layer details

    • Each block: Conv 3×3 (same padding, ReLU) → Conv 3×3 (valid padding, ReLU) → MaxPool 2×2.
    • Filters double every block: 32 → 64 → 128 → 256 → 512.
    • Spatial size: 256 → 127 → 62 → 30 → 14 → 6.
    • Flatten gives 6 × 6 × 512 = 18,432 features.
    • No normalisation layer is baked in: the model expects raw pixel values 0–255.
    • Dropout and other training-only layers are removed in the exported graph.
    05 · Inference

    From photo to treatment plan.

    Everything below runs on the visitor's device. No image is ever uploaded to a server.

    1. Image in

      A photo arrives by file picker, drag and drop, or one of the six sample leaves. Uploads are also saved as a downscaled (≤ 768 px) JPEG in localStorage so progress survives leaving the page.

    2. Pre-process

      tf.browser.fromPixels reads the pixels, resizeNearestNeighbor([256, 256]) resizes them, and the tensor is cast to float32 with a batch dimension — values stay in the 0–255 range.

    3. Predict

      The graph model runs on the GPU through WebGL inside tf.tidy, so intermediate tensors are freed automatically. A warm-up prediction right after loading makes the first real result fast.

    4. Rank

      The 38 softmax probabilities are sorted. The top class becomes the diagnosis with its confidence; the next two are shown as "other possibilities".

    5. Explain

      The raw label (e.g. Corn_(maize)___Common_rust_) is turned into a readable crop and condition, then matched to its treatment plan: summary, action plan and recommended products.

    06 · Delivery

    Making a 124 MB model feel instant.

    The weights are kept at full float32 precision, so the site focuses on downloading them once and early.

    Loading strategy

    • Background preload: once the home page has loaded, the runtime and all 31 shards download at low priority.
    • Service worker cache: the files are stored in Cache Storage, so later visits load the model from disk, not the network.
    • Prerendering: in Chrome and Edge the whole diagnosis page is prepared invisibly, so opening it is instant.
    • Instant samples: the six sample leaves have pre-computed results from this model, shown while the live model is still downloading.

    Measured locally (desktop)

    Measured on localhost with the model cached. A first visit also has to download 124 MB, which depends on the connection (roughly a minute at 20 Mbit/s).

    07 · Data

    Trained on the PlantVillage dataset.

    The model's 38 labels match the PlantVillage dataset exactly — an open collection of 54,306 leaf images across 14 crops, published by Hughes & Salathé and used by Mohanty et al. (2016).

    PlantVillage photos show a single leaf on a plain background in controlled lighting. Models trained on it score very highly on held-out PlantVillage images — the original study reported up to 99.35% with GoogLeNet — and this model classified all 18 sample photos tested during development correctly with ≥ 99% confidence.
    No independent accuracy figure ships with this model. The same study found accuracy dropped sharply (to about 31%) on photos taken in different conditions, so expect lower accuracy on real field photos with busy backgrounds, multiple leaves or unusual lighting. The sample photos are likely from the training data, so they demonstrate the tool rather than measure it.
    08 · Treatment sources

    Where the treatment advice comes from.

    Each treatment plan (summary, action steps and named products) was compiled from university extension and IPM programmes. Brand names are examples — always check local registration and follow the product label.

    09 · Limitations

    Know what it can't do.

    • It can only answer with one of its 38 classes — a crop or disease it was never trained on will be forced into the closest match, often with high confidence.
    • It looks at one leaf. Fruit, stem, root and whole-plant symptoms are outside its scope.
    • Similar-looking problems (nutrient deficiencies, herbicide damage, sunscald) can be mistaken for diseases.
    • Confidence is the model's own score, not a calibrated probability of being right.
    • Treatment advice is general guidance. Product registrations, doses and waiting periods differ by country and crop — confirm with a local agricultural extension service before spraying.
